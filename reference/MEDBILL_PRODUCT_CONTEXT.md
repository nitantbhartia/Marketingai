# BillKarma -- Product Context

This document is the single source of truth for tone, scope, and language
when generating any user-facing content (articles, reports, in-app copy,
emails, social posts, etc.).

---

## What BillKarma Does

- Lets patients photograph or upload a medical bill and instantly extracts
  every line item (CPT code, description, quantity, charge).
- Compares each charge against Medicare allowable rates and regional
  benchmarks to identify potential overcharges.
- Detects common billing errors: duplicate charges, unbundling (billing
  component codes separately instead of a single comprehensive code),
  upcoding, and charges for services the patient did not receive.
- Produces a plain-language report that explains each finding, the
  potential savings, and what to do next.
- Generates a ready-to-use phone script patients can read when calling
  the hospital billing department to dispute a charge.
- Optionally negotiates directly with hospital billing on the patient's
  behalf via email, charging a percentage of documented savings (capped).
- Grades hospitals on pricing transparency, billing accuracy, and
  responsiveness to disputes.
- Publishes educational content to help patients understand medical
  billing, their rights, and how to advocate for themselves.

## What BillKarma Does NOT Do

- BillKarma is **not** a law firm and does not provide legal advice.
- BillKarma does **not** guarantee any specific reduction or outcome.
- BillKarma does **not** file insurance claims on the patient's behalf.
- BillKarma does **not** replace a licensed medical billing advocate or
  attorney for complex disputes, litigation, or collections defense.
- BillKarma does **not** access or store a patient's medical records,
  diagnoses, or protected health information beyond what appears on the
  bill itself.
- BillKarma does **not** verify whether a service was medically necessary;
  it only checks the billing accuracy and pricing.

## Approved Language

Use this language freely in all content:

| Concept | Approved phrasing |
|---|---|
| What we surface | "potential overcharges," "billing errors," "findings," "flagged items" |
| Savings | "potential savings," "estimated savings," "possible reduction" |
| Medicare comparison | "Medicare allowable rate," "Medicare benchmark," "what Medicare pays" |
| User action | "dispute," "question your bill," "call billing," "request an itemized bill" |
| Tone markers | "you have the right to," "it's worth asking," "many patients don't realize" |
| Our role | "informational tool," "billing transparency platform," "patient advocacy tool" |
| Data sourcing | "based on publicly available CMS data," "according to Medicare fee schedules" |

## Forbidden Language

Never use the following in any user-facing content:

| Forbidden | Why | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| "You were scammed" / "fraud" / "illegal" | Implies criminal intent we cannot prove | "potential overcharge," "billing discrepancy" |
| "You are owed" / "they owe you" | Implies a legal entitlement we cannot guarantee | "you may be able to reduce," "potential savings" |
| "Guaranteed savings" / "we guarantee" | No outcome can be guaranteed | "potential savings," "estimated" |
| "Sue" / "lawsuit" / "take legal action" | We are not attorneys | "consult an attorney if needed" |
| "Always" / "never" (in absolute medical/legal claims) | Over-generalizes | "in many cases," "typically" |
| "Exposed" / "exposed your data" (about hospitals) | Sensationalist | "this charge appears higher than the benchmark" |
| "HIPAA violation" (about our own process) | Invites regulatory confusion | Describe our data practices plainly |
| Profanity or sarcasm | Unprofessional | Maintain a respectful, empowering tone |

## Tone Guidelines

1. **Empowering, not alarming.** The patient should feel informed and
   capable of taking action, not scared or angry. Lead with what they can
   do, not how bad the situation is.

2. **Plain language first.** Write at an 8th-grade reading level. Define
   jargon inline on first use (e.g., "CPT code (the standard billing code
   for a medical service)"). Avoid Latin, legalese, or insurance jargon
   without explanation.

3. **Precise, not vague.** Always cite the specific CPT code, the exact
   Medicare rate, and the exact charged amount when available. Numbers
   build trust.

4. **Neutral toward providers.** Hospitals make billing mistakes; that
   does not make them villains. Frame findings as discrepancies to
   investigate, not accusations.

5. **Honest about limits.** If a finding is uncertain, say so.  Use
   confidence qualifiers ("likely," "appears to be," "may indicate")
   rather than definitive statements when the data is ambiguous.

6. **Action-oriented.** Every piece of content should end with a clear
   next step the reader can take: scan a bill, call billing, request an
   itemized statement, or learn more.

7. **Respectful of the reader's time.** Front-load the most important
   information. Use bullet points, headers, and short paragraphs. Long
   walls of text lose readers who are already stressed about a bill.

8. **Consistent voice.** BillKarma speaks as a knowledgeable, calm friend
   who has worked in healthcare billing -- not as a corporate brand, not
   as a medical professional, and not as a lawyer.
