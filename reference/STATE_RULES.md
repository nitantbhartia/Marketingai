## CALIFORNIA

### Total Loss Rules

- **Method:** Total Loss Formula (TLF)
- **Formula:** Cost of Repair + Salvage Value ≥ Actual Cash Value
- **No fixed percentage threshold** — California uses the formula approach
- **Key regulation:** Cal. Code Regs. Title 10, § 2695.8

### Settlement Must Include

- **Sales tax:** YES — mandatory. Settlement must include all applicable taxes and one-time fees for a comparable vehicle, whether or not a replacement is actually purchased. (Cal. Code Regs. § 2695.8(b)(1))
- **Registration/title/transfer fees:** YES — one-time fees incident to transfer of ownership must be included
- **License fees:** YES — computed based on remaining term of the loss vehicle's current registration
- **Comparable vehicle basis:** Settlement must be based on the actual cost of a "comparable automobile" available for retail purchase in the local market area within 90 days of the final offer

### Dispute Rights

- **35-day reopener:** If the insured cannot purchase a comparable vehicle for the settlement amount within 35 calendar days of receiving payment/offer, the insurer MUST reopen the claim file (Cal. Code Regs. § 2695.8(c))
- **Appraisal clause:** Most California policies include an appraisal clause. Each party selects an appraiser; if they can't agree, a neutral umpire decides. Cost is split equally (~$250/side)
- **Insurer must provide valuation report:** The insurer must provide a copy of the evaluation used to determine ACV
- **Payment timeline:** Insurer must tender undisputed portion within 30 calendar days (Cal. Code Regs. § 2695.7(h))

### Diminished Value

- **Third-party claims:** Allowed (file against at-fault driver's insurer)
- **First-party claims:** Heavily restricted. Generally NOT covered unless policy explicitly includes it. Case law (Ray v. Farmers, 1988) established that insurers who elect to repair are not liable for diminished value.
- **Statute of limitations:** 3 years from date of accident

### Key Watchouts

- California has NO fixed percentage threshold — don't cite one
- Insurers cannot deduct for vehicle condition unless documented as below-average for that year/make/model
- Adjustments from comparable vehicle cost must be "discernible, measurable, itemized, and specified"
- Salvage: If insured retains vehicle, settlement still includes sales tax (discounted by salvage value's tax portion)

### DOI Contact

- **Website:** insurance.ca.gov
- **Consumer hotline:** 1-800-927-4357
- **Complaint filing:** insurance.ca.gov/0800-consumer-complaint

## TEXAS

### Total Loss Rules

- **Method:** Total Loss Threshold (TLT)
- **Threshold:** 100% — repair costs must equal or exceed the vehicle's ACV
- **This is the highest threshold in the country** — means more marginal cases may NOT be totaled in Texas
- **Key regulation:** Tex. Ins. Code § 1952.301 et seq.

### Settlement Must Include

- **Sales tax:** State law does not specifically mandate sales tax inclusion in the statute. However, Texas DOI guidance and standard practice require insurers to include applicable taxes and fees. Always check policy language.
- **Registration/title/transfer fees:** Standard practice to include, but verify with policy
- **Key distinction:** Texas relies more on policy language than statutory mandates for many settlement components

### Dispute Rights

- **Appraisal clause:** Most Texas auto policies include an appraisal clause. Either party can invoke it.
- **DOI complaint:** Can file a complaint with the Texas Department of Insurance if insurer is acting in bad faith
- **No specific statutory reopener period** like California's 35-day window

### Diminished Value

- **Third-party claims:** Allowed — file against at-fault driver's insurer
- **First-party claims:** Generally NOT covered unless explicitly stated in the policy
- **Statute of limitations:** 2 years for property damage claims (Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 16.003)
- **Note:** Some sources cite 4 years — the 2-year limit applies to property damage specifically

### Key Watchouts

- 100% threshold means your car is less likely to be totaled in Texas than in most states
- This can work against consumers — a car that "should" be totaled might only be repaired, leaving hidden damage
- Texas is an at-fault state for auto insurance
- No personal income tax — but 6.25% state sales tax (plus local, up to 8.25% total)

### DOI Contact

- **Website:** tdi.texas.gov
- **Consumer helpline:** 1-800-252-3439

## FLORIDA

### Total Loss Rules

- **Method:** Total Loss Threshold (TLT)
- **Threshold:** 80% — vehicle is totaled if repair costs reach 80% of ACV
- **Key regulation:** Fla. Stat. § 319.30

### Settlement Must Include

- **Sales tax:** YES — Florida requires insurers to include sales tax in total loss settlements (standard practice, reinforced by DOI guidance)
- **Registration/title/transfer fees:** YES — transfer fees and tag fees should be included
- **Electronic filing fee:** Florida has an electronic title fee that should be included

### Dispute Rights

- **Appraisal clause:** Available in most Florida policies
- **Mediation:** Florida DOI offers a free mediation program for claim disputes
- **DOI complaint process:** Florida has one of the more active state insurance departments for consumer complaints
- **No specific statutory reopener period**

### Diminished Value

- **Third-party claims:** Allowed — file against at-fault driver's insurer
- **First-party claims:** Generally NOT permitted unless explicitly stated in the policy
- **Statute of limitations:** 4 years for property damage (was 5 years, changed in 2024 tort reform — verify current law)
- **Note:** Florida is a no-fault state for bodily injury, but property damage claims follow at-fault rules

### Key Watchouts

- Florida is a NO-FAULT state for bodily injury but AT-FAULT for property damage — this confuses many consumers
- PIP (Personal Injury Protection) is required but separate from total loss property claims
- 80% threshold is relatively consumer-friendly
- Florida has high rates of uninsured drivers (~20%+) — UM/UIM coverage matters
- Hurricane/flood damage has separate considerations

### DOI Contact

- **Website:** floir.com
- **Consumer helpline:** 1-877-693-5236

## NEW YORK

### Total Loss Rules

- **Method:** Total Loss Threshold (TLT)
- **Threshold:** 75% — vehicle totaled when repair costs reach 75% of ACV
- **Key regulation:** NY Ins. Law § 2610; 11 NYCRR 216 (Reg. 64)

### Settlement Must Include

- **Sales tax:** YES — standard practice to include sales tax on replacement vehicle
- **Registration/title/transfer fees:** YES
- **Inspection fees:** New York requires vehicle inspection — this fee should be considered

### Dispute Rights

- **Appraisal clause:** Available in most New York policies
- **NY DFS complaint:** Can file complaint with NY Department of Financial Services
- **Prompt payment:** New York has strict prompt payment regulations for insurers

### Diminished Value

- **Third-party claims:** Allowed but difficult to pursue in practice
- **First-party claims:** Generally NOT recognized
- **Statute of limitations:** 3 years for property damage

### Key Watchouts

- New York is a no-fault state for bodily injury
- Property damage follows at-fault rules
- High cost of living means ACV and replacement costs tend to be higher
- New York City has additional considerations (parking, urban pricing premiums)

### DOI Contact

- **Website:** dfs.ny.gov
- **Consumer hotline:** 1-800-342-3736

## ILLINOIS

### Total Loss Rules

- **Method:** Total Loss Formula (TLF)
- **Formula:** Cost of Repair + Salvage Value ≥ ACV
- **Special rule for flood:** Vehicles submerged above the dashboard are automatically totaled. Flood damage vehicles totaled at 50% of ACV threshold.
- **Key regulation:** 215 ILCS 5/155.22a; 50 Ill. Admin. Code § 919

### Settlement Must Include

- **Sales tax:** YES — if the insured purchases or leases a replacement vehicle within 30 days, insurer must cover applicable sales tax, transfer, and title fees
- **Registration/title fees:** YES
- **30-day purchase requirement:** Sales tax coverage is contingent on replacement purchase within 30 days

### Dispute Rights

- **Appraisal clause:** Available in most policies
- **Illinois DOI complaint process available**

### Diminished Value

- **Third-party claims:** Allowed
- **First-party claims:** Generally NOT covered
- **Statute of limitations:** 5 years for property damage

### Key Watchouts

- The 30-day window for sales tax recovery is critical — consumers must act quickly
- Illinois has separate flood vehicle rules (lower threshold)
- Cook County/Chicago may have additional local fees
- Illinois is an at-fault state

### DOI Contact

- **Website:** insurance.illinois.gov
- **Consumer helpline:** 1-866-445-5364

## OHIO

### Total Loss Rules

- **Method:** Total Loss Formula (TLF)
- **Formula:** Cost of Repair + Salvage Value ≥ ACV
- **No fixed percentage threshold**
- **Key regulation:** Ohio Admin. Code § 3901-1-54

### Settlement Must Include

- **Sales tax:** YES — if insured provides documentation of replacement vehicle purchase within 30 days, insurer must cover applicable taxes, license fees, and other fees
- **Registration/title/transfer fees:** YES (with 30-day purchase documentation)
- **Note:** Insurer is only required to pay sales tax based on claim amount, not the cost of replacement vehicle

### Dispute Rights

- **Appraisal clause:** Available in most policies
- **Ohio DOI complaint process available**

### Diminished Value

- **Third-party claims:** Allowed
- **First-party claims:** Generally NOT covered
- **Statute of limitations:** 4 years for property damage

### Key Watchouts

- 30-day purchase documentation requirement for sales tax recovery
- Sales tax capped at the claim amount, not the replacement vehicle price
- Ohio is an at-fault state

### DOI Contact

- **Website:** insurance.ohio.gov
- **Consumer helpline:** 1-800-686-1526

## GEORGIA

### Total Loss Rules

- **Method:** Total Loss Formula (TLF)
- **Formula:** Cost of Repair + Salvage Value ≥ ACV
- **Additional rule:** Vehicle is also a total loss if 2 or more major component parts need replacement
- **Key regulation:** O.C.G.A. § 33-34-6; Ga. Comp. R. & Regs. 120-2-52

### Settlement Must Include

- **Sales tax:** YES — Georgia requires inclusion of applicable taxes and fees
- **Tag/title/transfer fees:** YES
- **Ad valorem tax:** Georgia has a title ad valorem tax (TAVT) that should be factored in

### Dispute Rights

- **Appraisal clause:** Available in most policies
- **Georgia DOI complaint process available**

### Diminished Value — GEORGIA IS UNIQUE

- **Third-party claims:** Allowed
- **First-party claims:** YES — Georgia is the ONLY state that requires insurers to proactively consider and pay diminished value on first-party claims (State Farm v. Mabry, 274 Ga. 498, 2001)
- **Insurer obligation:** Insurers must assess diminished value on ALL claims — policyholders don't even need to file a separate claim
- **17c Formula:** Georgia insurers commonly use the "Rule 17c" formula (cap at 10% of NADA value × damage multiplier × mileage multiplier). This formula often significantly undervalues DV — consumers can challenge with independent appraisals.
- **Statute of limitations:** 4 years for property damage

### Key Watchouts

- Georgia's first-party diminished value rights are the strongest in the nation — this is a major content angle
- The 17c formula is widely criticized as unfair to consumers
- Georgia is an at-fault state
- Title ad valorem tax (TAVT) is ~6.6% — can be a significant line item
- The 2+ major component parts rule is unique to Georgia

### DOI Contact

- **Website:** oci.georgia.gov
- **Consumer helpline:** 1-800-656-2298

## NORTH CAROLINA

### Total Loss Rules

- **Method:** Total Loss Threshold (TLT)
- **Threshold:** 75% of ACV
- **Key regulation:** N.C. Gen. Stat. § 20-109.1

### Settlement Must Include

- **Sales tax:** YES — standard practice
- **Registration/title/transfer fees:** YES
- **Highway use tax:** North Carolina charges a highway use tax on vehicle purchases (3% of value, capped)

### Dispute Rights

- **Appraisal clause:** Available in most policies
- **NC DOI complaint process available**
- **NC DOI is considered one of the more consumer-friendly departments**

### Diminished Value

- **Third-party claims:** Allowed
- **First-party claims:** YES — North Carolina is one of the few states (along with Georgia and Kansas) that allows first-party diminished value claims
- **Statute of limitations:** 3 years for property damage

### Key Watchouts

- First-party DV rights make NC a strong content target (along with GA and KS)
- Highway use tax is a unique line item
- North Carolina is an at-fault state with contributory negligence (if you're even 1% at fault, you may recover nothing in a third-party claim)
- Contributory negligence rule is extremely important to mention in NC content

### DOI Contact

- **Website:** ncdoi.gov
- **Consumer helpline:** 1-855-408-1212

## MICHIGAN

### Total Loss Rules

- **Method:** Total Loss Threshold (TLT)
- **Threshold:** 75% of ACV
- **Key regulation:** Mich. Comp. Laws § 257.217c

### Settlement Must Include

- **Sales tax:** YES — standard practice, 6% state sales tax
- **Registration/title/transfer fees:** YES
- **Plate transfer fee:** Michigan allows plate transfers — fee should be covered

### Dispute Rights

- **Appraisal clause:** Available in most policies
- **Michigan DIFS complaint process available**

### Diminished Value

- **Third-party claims:** Heavily restricted under Michigan's no-fault system and mini-tort law
- **First-party claims:** NOT recognized
- **Mini-tort limitation:** Michigan's mini-tort law limits property damage claims against at-fault drivers to $3,000 (as of current law — this amount may have been adjusted)
- **Statute of limitations:** 3 years

### Key Watchouts

- Michigan is a NO-FAULT state — this fundamentally changes the total loss process
- Mini-tort $3,000 cap is very restrictive for property damage claims against other drivers
- Diminished value is essentially not available in Michigan
- Michigan has some of the highest auto insurance rates in the country
- Recent no-fault reform may have changed some rules — verify current law

### DOI Contact

- **Website:** michigan.gov/difs
- **Consumer helpline:** 1-877-999-6442

## Universal Line Items (Apply in All States)

These items are commonly missed regardless of state:

1. **Sales tax on replacement vehicle** — Required or standard in virtually all states. Often the single largest missed item.
1. **Title and registration fees** — Transfer of ownership fees for the replacement vehicle.
1. **Comparable vehicle adjustments** — If the insurer's comps have higher mileage, worse condition, or fewer options than your vehicle, the ACV should be adjusted upward.
1. **Aftermarket upgrades** — Custom wheels, audio systems, window tint, performance modifications — often completely ignored in settlement calculations.
1. **Loss of use** — Compensation for time without a vehicle beyond what rental coverage provides. Not guaranteed in all states but worth pursuing.
1. **Dealer documentation fees** — If you'll need to buy from a dealer, doc fees ($100–$800 depending on state) are a legitimate cost.
1. **Emissions/safety inspection fees** — Required in many states for newly registered vehicles.

## Content Compliance Rules

When writing state-specific content:

1. **Always cite the specific state** — never say "most states require…" without listing which ones
1. **Include statute/code references** when available
1. **Use "may be entitled to" not "are entitled to"** unless the statute is crystal clear
1. **Always recommend consulting a local attorney** for complex situations
1. **Note that regulations change** — include a "last verified" date
1. **Link to the state DOI website** as a primary resource
1. **Never extrapolate one state's rules to another** — even neighboring states can have drastically different rules
1. **Flag no-fault vs. at-fault distinction** — this fundamentally changes how claims work
1. **For diminished value content:** Always clarify first-party vs. third-party distinction. Only GA, NC, and KS clearly allow first-party DV claims.
1. **For sales tax content:** Note any purchase-window requirements (IL and OH require 30-day documentation)
